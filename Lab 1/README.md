In the original stage production of Peter Pan, Tinker Bell was represented by a darting light created by a small handheld mirror off-stage, reflecting a little circle of light from a powerful lamp. Tinkerbell communicates her presence through this light to the other characters. See more info [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinker_Bell). 

There is no actor that plays Tinkerbell--her existence in the play comes from the interactions that the other characters have with her.

## Part A. Plan 

To stage an interaction with your interactive device, think about:

_Setting:_ Where is this interaction happening? (e.g., a jungle, the kitchen) When is it happening?

_Players:_ Who is involved in the interaction? Who else is there? If you reflect on the design of current day interactive devices like the Amazon Alexa, it’s clear they didn’t take into account people who had roommates, or the presence of children. Think through all the people who are in the setting.

_Activity:_ What is happening between the actors?

_Goals:_ What are the goals of each player? (e.g., jumping to a tree, opening the fridge). 

The interactive device can be anything *except* a computer, a tablet computer or a smart phone, but the main way it interacts needs to be using light.

\*\***Describe your setting, players, activity and goals here.**\*\*

Storyboards are a tool for visually exploring a users interaction with a device. They are a fast and cheap method to understand user flow, and iterate on a design before attempting to build on it. Take some time to read through this explanation of [storyboarding in UX design](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2017/10/storyboarding-ux-design/). Sketch seven storyboards of the interactions you are planning. **It does not need to be perfect**, but must get across the behavior of the interactive device and the other characters in the scene.

1. Morse Code Communicator
   Setting: Two living spaces and when both owners of the device are home
   Players: The two owners of the devices, their roommates, family, guests who visit, pets, other appliances or household objects that make sound
   Activity: The user says a small phrase to the device. The device picks up on both the phrase and the user's mood through tone and sends this info to another device that blinks in morse code in the color reflecting the     detected mood
   Goals:
   - To communicate in a fun non-verbal way  
   - Be able to communicate the mood to those who may have difficulty detecting it 
   - Encourage maintaining bonds across distances
   Storyboard:
   <img width="1058" height="662" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9fc709de-5467-4377-adf9-06414dfb1e98" />



2. Sound to Image Device
   Setting: The device is portable and the setting can be anywhere within the vicinity of where the user decides to bring it and is used when the user wants to know what a sound may look like
   Players: Anyone or anything that makes sounds within the radius of the device (ie. user, passerbys, wind, animals, machines, background noises
   Activity: The user makes a sound or puts the device near a sound, the device reflects the sound as colors
   Goals:
   - Translate the experience of people with synesthesia
   - Share auditory experiences with those with auditory impairments
   - Be able to see sound in a different way
   Storyboard:
   <img width="720" height="675" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/faf11269-3575-4233-9f1a-5f11873fc9ca" />


3. Odor Device
   Setting: This is a wearable device and thus the setting is anywhere the user goes (it is located on the user's body and always in use)
   Players: The user, any people, animals, external scents in the vicinity
   Activity: The user wears the device which indicates the current user's odor state. If the user smells good then the device will indicate a green color. If the user smells like nothing or slightly bad, then the device    will indicate a yellow warning indicator. If the user smells bad, then the device will indicate a red color.
   Goals:
   - To let the user know if they smell
   - To detect other smells in the vicinity
   - To help those with weaker senses of smell to detect bad odors
   Storyboard:
   <img width="1134" height="394" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/24be6555-3aae-4a4b-9141-c754399ed966" />

  
5. Task Reminder System
   Setting: The home of the user
   Players: Any person or animal in the owner's home (the user, family, guests, pets, etc.)
   Activity: The device turns certain colors at certain times to remind user to do different tasks (will not turn off unless user completes task or overrides)
   Goals: 
   - Help users do daily tasks in a regular routinely manner
   - Encourage a more visual sense of reminding and a less easy way to ignore daily task reminders
   Storyboard:
   <img width="920" height="584" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/58d688bc-d56c-40d3-ae63-b5fbdd655005" />

  
6. Weekly Budgeting Manager
   Setting: The home of the user
   Players: Any person/animal in the owner's house who can walk byh and see the light
   Activity: The device has a couple of categories with a column of bulbs (around 5 bulbs each). Each category is set with a budget for the week. As long as each budget is above 75% of the allocated budget, the overall   light of the pig board is green as well as each individual column light. When at least one category falls below 75% of budget, the pig board turns yellow and that indiviudal column lights turn yellow (with the bulb lights that are still on representing the percentage of the budget remaining. The other categories that are above 75% will still be green. When at least one category falls below 25%, the board turns red with the individual remaining column lights for that column being red. If the spending exceeds the budget for a column then the label for that column will blink a flashing red to signal that column is now in the negatives).
   Goals:
   - To help users keep track of spending in a less ignorable manner
   - To help users understand where the majority of their spending is going and figure out and target places to cut down
   - To help users stick to a goal of what to spend on a weekly basis
   Storyboard:
